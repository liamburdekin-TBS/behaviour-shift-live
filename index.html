What is Behaviour Shift?

Change the lens before you change the behaviour.

Behaviour Shift starts with a simple challenge: if we only focus on stopping what we can see, we can miss what the behaviour is trying to communicate.

The work turns the lens back towards the adult too. How we arrive, regulate, interpret and respond all become part of what happens next.

About Behaviour Shift

Changing the response. Changing the outcome.

Behaviour Shift exists to help the adults around children understand behaviour more deeply, reflect on the part their own response plays in what happens next, and create stronger, safer and more consistent ways forward.

It is not about choosing sides.
It is about bringing people together around one shared goal: better outcomes for children.
Why Behaviour Shift exists

People can care about the same child and still see the same moment very differently.

Parents carry love, history and emotion. Professionals carry responsibility, safeguarding and accountability. Residential staff carry the reality of twenty-four-hour care. Teachers and wider services see another part of the picture. Each perspective can be valid — but when those perspectives become disconnected, it is often the child who experiences the inconsistency.

Parents & carers

History, attachment, fear, hope and the reality of loving a child through difficult moments.

Residential teams

Daily relationships, boundaries, risk, routines and the pressure of responding in the moment.

Professionals

Safeguarding, planning, accountability, evidence and responsibility for difficult decisions.

The child

Experiencing all of those responses at once — and trying to make sense of what the adults around them mean.

The Behaviour Shift position

The answer is not to decide which adult is “right”. It is to create a shared language around behaviour, make room for different perspectives and help the adults around the child respond with greater understanding, confidence and consistency.

How it was created
Lived experience met frontline practice. Behaviour Shift was not created from theory alone. It grew from experiencing services as a child, then later working inside children’s care and seeing the same moments from the adult side of the relationship.

The thinking behind Behaviour Shift began much earlier than the name. Its founder grew up around instability, addiction, exploitation, violence and street involvement. Those experiences created an understanding of what behaviour can look like from the inside: the choices that make little sense to an adult can feel completely logical to the young person living them.

Later, frontline work with children and young people — across residential care, safeguarding, missing episodes, exploitation, self-harm, crisis situations and multi-agency working — created a second perspective. The question was no longer only “Why is the child doing this?” It also became “What are we, as adults, bringing into the moment?”

That combination matters. Behaviour Shift is not built from standing outside the system and criticising it. It comes from having been affected by the system, then working within it and seeing how much responsibility adults carry when situations are complex, emotional and high risk.

Again and again, one pattern stood out: adults could genuinely want the best for a child and still respond from completely different interpretations of the same behaviour. One person sees defiance. Another sees distress. Another sees risk. Another sees rejection. Another sees a problem that needs solving immediately.

The behaviour has not changed. The lens has. And once the lens changes, the adult response often changes with it.

That became the foundation of Behaviour Shift:
If we can understand what sits beneath behaviour, notice what pressure brings out in us, and become more intentional about our response, we create more opportunity for a different outcome.
Our purpose

Understanding that changes what happens next.

Behaviour Shift turns reflection into practical change. It brings lived experience, frontline knowledge and interactive learning together so adults can look beneath behaviour without removing boundaries, responsibility or safeguarding.

01 · Understand

Look beneath behaviour.

Move beyond the surface action and explore context, communication, pressure, need, risk, influence and what the behaviour may be achieving for the child.

02 · Reflect

Turn the lens around.

Notice how adult assumptions, emotions, pressure, language and professional roles can shape the response a child receives.

03 · Respond

Choose what helps next.

Build responses that hold boundaries and safeguarding while protecting connection, increasing consistency and creating a more useful route forward.

What we believe

Behaviour is communication. But understanding is only useful if it changes the response.

Every behaviour has context.
The visible action is rarely the whole story.
Connection and boundaries can exist together.
Understanding behaviour does not mean excusing harmful or unsafe choices.
Adults deserve support, not judgement.
Parents, carers and professionals are often responding under pressure too.
Different language can carry the same meaning.
Shared understanding helps different parts of the system work together.
The adult response is part of the environment.
Tone, pace, certainty, curiosity and regulation can all influence what happens next.
Small shifts can change trajectories.
A different response in one important moment can create space for a different decision, relationship or outcome.

The behaviour you are seeing may already be part of something bigger.

The latest Youth Endowment Fund research shows how strongly these risks can overlap. Among 13–17-year-olds who said they had been in a ‘gang’, 55% also transported or hid drugs, weapons or money when approached, and 39% had been missing at least monthly.

55%of teenagers reporting ‘gang’ involvement transported or hid drugs, weapons or money when approached
39%of those reporting ‘gang’ involvement had been missing at least monthly
32%of teenagers in ‘gangs’ or being exploited reported carrying a weapon

Class A drug use showed the same concentration of risk: it was reported by 29% of teenagers frequently missing from home, 52% of those reporting ‘gang’ involvement and 50% of those who were criminally exploited.

Behaviour Shift starts where the parent often already is

You may already have had the arguments. The phone may already have been taken. Police, school, social care or other professionals may already be involved. Your child may already know exactly what everyone wants them to stop doing.

The next question becomes: what is keeping the behaviour powerful? Money? Status? Protection? Debt? Fear? Drugs? Loyalty? Belonging? A relationship? The feeling that there is no safe way back?

Understanding the pull does not mean accepting the risk. It gives us a better place from which to challenge it.

Source: Youth Endowment Fund, Children, Violence and Vulnerability 2025 — Exploitation and gangs. ‘Gang’ reflects the survey’s own defined term.

Behaviour Shift Evidence Library · Parents & CarersResponse · Relationship · Change

They can be at risk and taking risks at the same time.

Young people involved in violence do not fit neatly into “victim” or “offender”. In YEF’s 2025 research, 53% of teenagers who had perpetrated violence had also been victims of violence. Among those whose violence caused an injury requiring medical treatment, 77% had themselves been victims.

53%of teenagers who perpetrated violence had also experienced violence as a victim
77%of those causing serious injury had themselves been victims of violence
39%of serious cases reported receiving support to help stop it happening again

That does not remove responsibility. It changes the question. A child carrying a knife may also be frightened. A child dealing drugs may also be exploited. A child going missing may be running towards something, away from something, or both. A child becoming aggressive at home may be carrying pressure that began somewhere else.

This is the space Behaviour Shift works in

How do I keep a boundary without losing the relationship?
How do I challenge the behaviour without reducing my child to the behaviour?
How do I recognise exploitation without removing all responsibility?
How do I respond when they come home after going missing?
How do I stay connected when I am angry, frightened and exhausted too?

Behaviour Shift helps parents move from repeatedly proving that the choice is dangerous towards understanding why the young person still believes it is worth the gamble — and how a different adult response can begin creating another route.

Professional training pathway

One-Day Crash Course.

The Crash Course is the bridge into Behaviour Shift training: a practical, interactive day that creates a shared foundation around the adult response under pressure — mindset, Pressure Profiles, team culture, behaviour as communication, reframing, PACE, Socratic questioning, boundaries, repair and reinforcement.

From there, the next stage is tailored. Behaviour Shift can build follow-on sessions around the home’s current needs — the patterns staff are seeing, the risks children are facing, team culture, communication, safeguarding themes or specific areas of practice that need strengthening. The aim is not one fixed package for every home, but a training pathway shaped around what that home needs now.

Where Behaviour Shift fits

Behaviour Shift strengthens the thinking that happens before the response: understanding behaviour as communication, staying curious before reacting, de-escalation, communication under pressure, reflective practice, consistent adult responses, relationship before redirection and staff confidence when situations escalate.

As at 31 March 2026, around 20% of children’s homes in England did not have a registered manager in post. In the North West, the figure was approximately 22%.

1 in 5children’s homes in England without a registered manager in post

Those regulatory cases included child-protection concerns, compliance and enforcement activity, and inspections being brought forward. The evidence establishes the pressure surrounding the sector — it does not mean homes or managers are failing.

For managers and providers

The challenge is not simply compliance. It is creating teams that can think consistently, communicate effectively and respond to behaviour with confidence — even when staffing, risk and operational pressure are high.

Behaviour Shift is designed to complement existing safeguarding and mandatory training by strengthening staff confidence, team culture, consistency of response, reflective leadership, communication between staff, less reactive practice, shared understanding of behaviour and a common language across the home.

Behaviour Shift Live

The room becomes part of the training.

Behaviour Shift Live is the interactive layer running through Behaviour Shift sessions. Participants reflect privately, contribute anonymously, respond to scenarios, explore their own reactions under pressure and see the thinking of the wider room become part of the discussion.

The facilitator does not just deliver information.
They work with what the room is thinking.
How a session works

From private thought to shared learning.

01Join

Participants enter the Behaviour Shift Live room using a room code or QR code.

02Think

A question, scenario or situation is introduced. People get space to think before discussion begins.

03Respond

Participants contribute anonymously through their own device.

04Reveal

The room’s responses become visible and form part of the discussion.

05Reflect

The facilitator explores differences in perspective, assumptions and adult responses.

06Shift

The learning is connected back to what people may do differently in the real moment.

Think first. Respond honestly. Discuss together. Shift the response.
Shared Wall

See what the room is thinking.

Participants respond anonymously before discussion opens up. The facilitator reveals the room, spots themes and uses the responses to explore different perspectives in real time.
