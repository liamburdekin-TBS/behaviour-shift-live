Why Behaviour Shift Exists

Same goal.
Different pressures.
Too many gaps.

Tap each group to explore what they may be carrying into the room — and why the same situation can look completely different from each side.

FamiliesHistory, emotion and fear of being judged

Parents and relatives may bring years of history, love, fear, frustration and exhaustion into every conversation. They may feel that professionals only see their hardest moments, while missing the full story of the child and family.

They can also feel powerless when decisions are made around them, particularly when they do not fully understand the process, language or expectations being placed upon them.

What might look like resistance when it is actually fear, shame or feeling unheard?

ProfessionalsRisk, responsibility and difficult decisions

Social workers, residential staff, foster carers and support professionals carry legal duties, safeguarding responsibilities, organisational procedures and accountability for decisions that may have serious consequences.

They may be balancing immediate risk against long-term relationships, while working with incomplete information, limited time and pressure from several directions at once.

What might look like coldness or control when it is actually responsibility, uncertainty or fear of getting it wrong?

ChildrenLiving inside the consequences of adult decisions

Children experience every disagreement, delay, handover and change of plan in real life. They may hear different messages from different adults and feel expected to manage emotions or systems they did not create.

When adults work separately, children can become the messenger, the evidence or the problem. Their behaviour may then communicate confusion, divided loyalty, fear, grief or a need to regain some control.

What is the child carrying because the adults around them are not yet connected?

Behaviour Shift is not about choosing a side. It is about helping every side see the whole child.

Session One

We are here for
the same child.

A shared room for parents, grandparents, kinship carers, social workers, residential staff, foster carers and family support professionals.

Tap one of the coloured groups above to reveal their perspective without leaving this page.
Parents

Parents know their child’s history, personality, relationships and life outside professional involvement. They may also carry worry, guilt, judgement or fear of being misunderstood.

What might parents need from the other adults in the room?

Kinship carers & grandparents

They may step into a parenting role suddenly while managing family loyalties, disrupted relationships and responsibility they never expected. They can protect identity and belonging while needing support themselves.

What pressures might be invisible to everyone else?

Social workers

Social workers hold safeguarding, legal duties, risk, family history and the wider plan at the same time. Their decisions can carry serious consequences, often under limited time and heavy caseload pressure.

What information or responsibility might they be carrying that others cannot see?

Residential care staff

Residential staff see the child’s daily patterns, routines, crises, relationships and recovery after difficult moments. They balance immediate safety with preserving trust and connection.

What can their day-to-day experience add to the wider plan?

Foster carers

Foster carers provide family life while working within professional plans, contact arrangements and uncertainty. They may be asked to build attachment while knowing change or transition could come quickly.

What support helps foster carers care without feeling isolated?

Family support professionals

Family support teams often see practical barriers up close: routines, housing, attendance, finances, confidence and engagement. They help turn plans into realistic everyday change.

What makes a plan genuinely workable for a family?
